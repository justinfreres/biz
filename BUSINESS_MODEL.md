# FlowBridge Systems LLC — Business Model

## The business in one sentence

FlowBridge Systems is an independent senior-practitioner consultancy that helps organizations stabilize, upgrade, migrate, and support the business software their workflows and operations depend on: K2, Nintex, and Odoo.

## Brand and category

**Working legal name:** FlowBridge Systems LLC  
**Public descriptor:** Workflow & ERP Reliability Consulting  
**Tagline:** Make business-critical software reliable again.  
**Founder positioning:** Former K2-certified support specialist; K2 Five: Core certified; senior software engineer with documented Odoo development and production-support experience.

“FlowBridge” earns its place because the company bridges old and new workflow platforms, software and operations, and urgent support with a stable long-term plan. It is intentionally not tied to a vendor. Confirm name, trademark, and domain availability before treating it as final.

## Ideal clients

Focus on clients where a broken platform interrupts a meaningful business process and an internal team lacks enough senior coverage.

| Segment | Problem to solve | Buyer | First offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| K2/Nintex customers | Aging workflow estate, blocked upgrade or cloud transition, scarce K2 knowledge | CIO, IT director, applications manager, SharePoint/workflow lead | Stability Audit |
| Odoo users | Fragile customizations, backlog, integration failures, upgrade risk, no reliable technical owner | COO, finance/operations leader, IT manager, Odoo product owner | Stability Audit |
| Implementation partners/MSPs | Need senior escalation capacity without hiring full-time | Delivery director, practice lead, account executive | Recovery Sprint or white-label retainer |

Avoid trying to be general IT support, a low-cost help desk, or a broad “digital transformation” firm. The valuable lane is high-context platform continuity.

## Engagement ladder

The ladder turns cold leads into a paid diagnostic before exposing the business to an undefined rescue project.

| Offer | Client outcome | Scope control | Suggested launch price |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 30-minute fit call | Mutual go/no-go decision | No troubleshooting; collect context only | No charge |
| Stability Audit | Risk register, architecture snapshot, prioritized roadmap, estimate | 10-hour target; paid in advance; no production changes unless added in writing | $1,500–$2,000 |
| Recovery Sprint | Defined repair, migration-plan, upgrade, or integration outcome | Written assumptions, acceptance criteria, change budget, 2–4 week window | From $4,500 |
| Continuity Retainer | Predictable senior support and release capacity | 10/20-hour monthly blocks; 3-month initial term; hours expire only as agreed | From $1,800/month |
| Emergency response | Rapid expert stabilization | Availability only, remote-first, written hourly cap | $195/hour, 8-hour minimum |

Start ordinary hourly work at **$150/hour**. These are launch recommendations, not a promise to clients. A 2026 U.S. Odoo market review places senior North American consultants around $100–$200+ hourly; use your first three paid projects to calibrate against conversion, margin, and scope quality rather than racing downward on price. ## Delivery model

1. **Qualify:** Is the platform in scope, is there a business owner, is the problem material, and can the client fund an audit?
2. **Diagnose:** Run a paid Stability Audit; interview stakeholders; review architecture, incidents, backlog, security, data, and release process.
3. **Commit:** Deliver an executive readout with a ranked 30/60/90-day plan. Propose the smallest Recovery Sprint that removes the highest risk.
4. **Recover:** Execute to written acceptance criteria. Every production change has client approval, test evidence, a rollback plan, and release notes.
5. **Retain:** Offer a Continuity Retainer only after the client sees the value of senior, reserved capacity.

Use milestone-based fixed pricing when the result and assumptions are clear. Use time-and-materials with a not-to-exceed cap when discovery is still in progress. Never give an all-in migration quote from a conversation.

## Revenue model and first target

The first objective is not a large pipeline; it is repeatable evidence that the paid-audit-to-sprint motion works.

| Monthly mix example | Revenue |
|---|---:|
| 2 Stability Audits at $1,500 | $3,000 |
| 2 Core Retainers at $1,800 | $3,600 |
| 1 Recovery Sprint at $4,500 | $4,500 |
| **Illustrative monthly billings** | **$11,100** |

Set a 90-day target of one paid audit in month one, one Recovery Sprint in month two, and two retainers by the end of month three. Track cash received, not just proposals sent. Review monthly with an accountant to set an appropriate owner draw, tax reserve, and operating reserve.

## Sales motion

### The first 30 prospects

- Former colleagues who have firsthand experience with your delivery quality
- K2/Nintex administrators, SharePoint leads, and implementation partners
- Odoo developers, functional consultants, and small Odoo partner firms that need escalation capacity
- Local and regional companies where past software/infrastructure relationships are credible

Each prospect receives a personal note, not a mass pitch: name the specific platform, state the outcome you help with, and invite a short fit call. Ask every completed audit for one introduction and a LinkedIn recommendation.

### Content that earns trust

Publish one short practical insight every two weeks on LinkedIn:

1. “What to inventory before a K2/Nintex migration conversation.”
2. “Three signs an Odoo customization is becoming an upgrade risk.”
3. “Why a support backlog needs an owner, not just tickets.”
4. “The release checklist I use before touching business-critical workflows.”

Do not publish client details, configuration screenshots, credentials, or vendor-confidential material.

## Operating guardrails

- Work under a signed services agreement and statement of work before access or changes.
- Use a client-provided account and named approver; never share credentials.
- Maintain written change control, test evidence, release notes, and a rollback plan.
- Take payment in advance for audits; bill retainers in advance; bill project milestones before the next phase.
- Do not claim vendor partnership, certification currency, or migration certification beyond verifiable facts.
- Ask a Missouri attorney and CPA to review entity, contract, tax, insurance, and data-security choices before launch.
