# Missouri LLC Setup Checklist

> This is an operational checklist, not legal, tax, or insurance advice. Use a Missouri business attorney and CPA for decisions about entity structure, contracts, tax elections, insurance, and privacy.

## Decision gate: do these before filing

- [ ] Confirm **FlowBridge Systems LLC** is available in the Missouri business search and is sufficiently distinct for your intended market.
- [ ] Decide whether the LLC will be member-managed (typical for a solo owner) or manager-managed after legal advice.
- [ ] Select a registered agent and a principal office address. Consider privacy implications before using a home address.
- [ ] Decide the final legal name, public name, domain, and email naming convention together.
- [ ] Obtain legal advice if you will serve regulated clients, process personal data, have subcontractors, or cross state lines.

The Missouri Secretary of State states that a limited liability company is formed by filing Articles of Organization and provides online registration and entity-name resources. [Missouri starting-a-business guidance](https://www.sos.mo.gov/business/startbusiness) · [Missouri filing resources](https://s1.sos.mo.gov/business/outreach/stepbystepguides)

## Formation sequence

1. **Clear the name.** Search Missouri’s entity records. Do not order a domain or print materials until you are comfortable with the legal/brand result.
2. **File the LLC.** File the Articles of Organization using Missouri’s official process. The Missouri FAQ currently states that online LLC filing is $50 versus $105 by paper; verify the fee at the moment of filing. [Missouri FAQ](https://www.sos.mo.gov/business/faqs)
3. **Save the proof.** Store the accepted Articles, state confirmation, and any operating agreement in a secure company folder.
4. **Get an EIN.** Form the state entity first, then use the IRS’s official EIN service. The IRS says the online application is free, must be completed in one session, and an approved EIN can be issued immediately. [IRS EIN guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)
5. **Create an operating agreement.** Even a single-member LLC should have a written operating agreement. Have an attorney review it.
6. **Open a business bank account.** Bring the state formation record, EIN confirmation, operating agreement if requested, and identification. Keep all business receipts and payments separate from personal accounts.
7. **Set accounting and tax process.** Ask a CPA about federal/state registrations, estimated taxes, deductible expenses, bookkeeping, owner draws, sales-tax applicability, and whether an S-corp election is appropriate for your facts.
8. **Buy the right insurance.** Request quotes for professional liability/errors & omissions, cyber liability, and general liability. Ask about contract requirements from target clients.
9. **Adopt commercial documents.** Have counsel review a master services agreement, statement of work, change order, confidentiality/data-protection language, and independent-contractor terms before client work.
10. **Set annual reminders.** Track state compliance, tax dates, insurance renewals, software subscriptions, certificate renewals, and contract renewals in Odoo or your calendar.

## Business setup checklist

- [ ] Domain and business email are owned by the LLC or recorded as reimbursed company property.
- [ ] LinkedIn profile and services page use the verified legal/business description.
- [ ] Public website has a legitimate contact route and a privacy notice before any lead form collects personal information.
- [ ] Odoo company profile, invoice header, payment terms, and tax settings have been reviewed by the CPA.
- [ ] Password manager, MFA, device encryption, backup, and incident-contact procedure are in place.
- [ ] Client access procedure prohibits shared passwords and requires named client approval for production changes.
- [ ] Contract templates prohibit unapproved subcontracting and define ownership, confidentiality, acceptance, and payment terms.

## Information to bring to your attorney/CPA

- Proposed legal name and owner information
- State of formation and home-office/registered-agent choices
- Expected first-year revenue mix: audits, projects, retainers, hourly emergency work
- Whether you will hire subcontractors or employees
- Whether clients will provide production access or regulated/personal data
- Whether you expect work in multiple states or countries
- Desired invoice/payment terms and overdue-work policy

## Do not skip

Do not commingle personal and business funds, accept production credentials by unencrypted email, use an unsigned scope for a rescue project, or promise a response time that has not been contracted and staffed.
